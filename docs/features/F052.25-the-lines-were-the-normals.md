# F052.25 — The lines on the body were one line of code

**Parent:** F052 · **Priority:** critical · 2026-08-28
**Reported by:** Christian — **correcting my reading of his original report.**

## What he actually meant, all along

> "Det var IKKE de skarpe overgange det var de linjer der er på kroppen af
> mennesket så det ser ud som om det er samlet — det var aldrig noget med
> farverne."

This morning he wrote *"lidt skarpe overgange i renderingen"* and **I decided what
that meant.** I read it as colour boundaries between regions, measured the
partition, wrote a confident plan-doc proving the model was fine and the *paint*
was hard-edged, and shipped a colour blend.

He meant **the lines on the skin.**

Every measurement in F052.21 was correct. All of them answered a question he had
not asked.

---

## The measurement

Same page, same props, same viewport. The only difference is one call.

| | result |
|---|---|
| **with** `geo.computeVertexNormals()` | a vertical line down the torso; horizontals across chest, waist, shoulders, knees, elbows, wrists, ankles |
| **without** it | **none of them.** Smooth body. |

**Why.** `computeVertexNormals()` averages face normals **per vertex index**. This
mesh has **split vertices along its UV seams** — two copies at the same position,
each averaging a different set of faces. The two sides of a seam therefore get
different normals and light differently, which is a visible crease running exactly
along the seam. The GLB ships correct smooth normals. We discarded them and
rebuilt worse ones.

---

## I had already found it, and talked myself out of it

F052.21's card, verbatim:

> *ALSO FOUND, not fixed here: `three.tsx:271` calls `geo.computeVertexNormals()`,
> discarding the normals the file ships. **Harmless on this mesh** (mostly welded —
> 12,010 vertices for 21,160 triangles), but it is authored data being thrown away
> for no stated reason. Noted, not touched.*

I called it harmless **on an arithmetic argument, without ever rendering it.**
"Mostly welded" is exactly the wrong inference: the seams are precisely the
minority of vertices that are *not* welded, and they are where every line appears.

So the thing I noticed and dismissed was the whole bug — while the change I shipped
instead cost the mark its WCAG contrast (F052.23).

> **An argument about the data is not a substitute for looking at the output.**

---

## The fix

```ts
if (!geo.getAttribute("normal")) geo.computeVertexNormals();
```

Kept as a **fallback**: a `.glb` with no normal attribute would otherwise render
flat. Costs nothing — the mark measures 4.71:1 before and after.

---

## What this pairs with

- **F052.23** turns the colour blend off by default (it broke AA).
- **This card** removes the lines the blend was trying to hide.

Together they land where he wanted to be this morning: **a smooth body and a
strong mark**, rather than a blurred body and a weak one.
