# F074.6 — Prune/TTL: the shape is knowable now, the numbers are not

**Status:** Backlog — shape only; numbers wait for September
**Parent:** F074 — `@broberg/notifications`
**Source:** fd-sundhed, 2026-08-29, from reading their OWN cleanup job to answer us

## Why this stayed open, and why that is still right

| consumer | prunes? | measured |
|---|---|---|
| xrt81 | no — calls the absence a gap | 304 rows in ~10 days, 15 people |
| cardmem | never | warned against designing a TTL from an assumption they have one |
| fd-sundhed | yes — 30 days, flat | 42 rows today; real figures arrive in September, 16,838 users |

Three orders of magnitude between the smallest and the largest. A default
lifetime chosen today would be chosen on a guess, and it would then be the
number every consumer inherits.

## What changed

fd-sundhed made the distinction that unblocks this: **the numbers need September;
the shape does not.** And they handed over two findings made while reading their
own `api/cron/notification-cleanup` to answer our question — measured in their
code, not remembered.

### 1 · A single age cutoff deletes the messages that still had an errand

Their job deletes everything where `created_at` is older than 30 days, **read or
unread**. So an unread notification vanishes on day 31 having never been seen.

That is backwards from what a notification *is*: the unread one is the one still
carrying a task. They found it answering us and said plainly it is probably
wrong.

**Shape decision: two numbers, not one.** Delete read after X, unread after Y.
And the API must not let a caller express "everything older than N" by accident —
if that is what they want, they should have to say it twice.

### 2 · A retention setting that looks like it works

`notification_auto_delete_days`, per user, **is ignored**. The code uses 30 for
everyone, with a comment saying it can be extended later. The user can set it,
the form saves it, and nothing reads it.

This is the shape this repo has now named six separate times in a week: **a field
that is accepted and does nothing**, indistinguishable from one that works. Their
rule for us is binary and correct — *honour the per-subscriber value, or do not
offer the field.*

## The reason there were two hand-rolled TTLs to find bugs in

Because this package has no prune. Worth writing down rather than treating their
code as a curiosity: the gap is what produced the implementations, and the
defects are what the gap costs.

## Decided here (shape)

- Read and unread expire on **separate clocks**.
- A per-subscriber retention value is **honoured or absent** — never accepted and
  ignored.
- Whatever prunes runs the **same choreography as every other mutation**: write,
  recount through the store's single counting function, announce. A prune that
  deletes counted rows without announcing is F074.5's defect on a schedule.

## Deliberately NOT decided here

- **X and Y.** September.
- **Whether the core prunes at all**, versus handing the consumer a predicate.
  cardmem never prunes; a core that assumes everyone does fits exactly one app —
  which is the trap the count query already taught this package once. The
  decision goes in this doc with its reason *before* any code.

## Rollout

1. Write the core-prunes-vs-consumer-prunes decision into this doc, with its
   reason. No code before it.
2. Quote fd-sundhed's September figures and xrt81's growth here. Then choose X
   and Y.
3. Build to the shape above, with the announce path proven by a store whose
   count cannot be derived by arithmetic on the pruned ids.
4. Mutation pass: collapsing the two lifetimes, and ignoring the per-subscriber
   value, must each turn a test red.
