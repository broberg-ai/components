# F052.23 — The seam blend pushed the mark BELOW WCAG AA; default it off

**Parent:** F052 · **Priority:** critical · 2026-08-28
**Reported by:** Christian, on production, on his iPhone. Relayed by fd-sundhed.

---

> et problem for svagtseende."

**Position: no.** **Colours: yes — and I shipped it without the approval I had

---

## The measurement

Same page, same props, same viewport (`iphone-15`), fd-sundhed's real palette,
one small mark (`knee_left`, intensity 9). The **only** difference between the two
builds is `SEAM` disabled in a control. Averaged over every pixel of the mark:

| build | mark avg | contrast vs body |
|---|---|---|
| **with the blend** (0.3.0 → 0.4.0) | `rgb(114, 58, 79)` | **3.59:1** — FAILS AA |
| without it (0.2.6 behaviour) | `rgb(111, 22, 56)` | **4.71:1** — passes |

Green `22→58`, blue `56→79`. The blend mixes the **body** colour into the mark —
precisely what it was designed to do at a boundary. The flaw is one of scale: **on
a small mark, the boundary is most of the mark.**

**Why a spot-check would have missed it:** at the single strongest pixel the cost
is 5.12 → 4.78, both above threshold. Only the AVERAGE crosses the line — and the
average is what a person sees.

**4.5:1 is not a preference here.** fd-sundhed's customer is Aalborg Kommune, a
public authority under the EU web accessibility directive, and 16,838 municipal
employees get this surface from 1 September. F052.19 established AA as a legal
requirement for this component. **This release broke the thing that card existed
to protect.**

## Two process failures, both mine

**1. I shipped a cosmetic change the owner never approved.** He asked to see the
new figure — *"Kan du smide en udgave af den nye figur i Downloads så jeg kan
godkende"* — I delivered the set, and he never answered. I published 0.3.0 anyway,
put it in the Discovery roster, and it reached a live public-health surface. The
request for approval was not a formality I had already satisfied by asking.

**2. I proved it in the one setting it does not ship into.** F052.21's own
constraint read: *"Prove it VISUALLY, before and after, on the same viewport."* I
did — on a **desktop** viewport, with the **default** palette, on **large** marked
regions. The regression needs a small mark, a phone, and a consumer's palette.
Every one of those three is the actual product.

> A visual proof is only evidence about the configuration it was taken in.

---

## The fix

`seam?: boolean` on `BodyMap3D`, **default `false`**.

- Off → the flat nearest-anchor paint 0.2.6 shipped. An existing consumer upgrades
  and gets their contrast back **with no code change**.
- On → the F052.21 soft transition, unchanged.

**Default off, deliberately** — the opposite of the reasoning in F052.22, and for
the opposite reason. There, a default-on reached people who would never read a
changelog. Here, the thing that must reach them without reading anything is the
**accessible** rendering.

**The blend is not deleted.** It is a real improvement on a large marked region,
Christian asked for it, and this is a scale problem, not a mistake in the idea.

## Reuse

Discovery searched for `contrast`, `wcag`, `accessibility`. One real hit:
**`@broberg/theme`** ships `checkContrastAA()` on its `/design-md` entry, and it
uses culori's `wcagContrast` internally.

**It cannot be reused as-is, and that is a gap in OUR package, not a reason to
hand-roll.** `checkContrastAA(content)` takes a DESIGN.md *string* and checks
four hard-coded token pairs (`on-surface`/`surface`, `foreground`/`background`,
`primary-foreground`/`primary`, `card-foreground`/`card`). It has no way to ask
"what is the ratio between these two arbitrary colours?", which is what a body
map needs — its pairs are palette colours and rendered pixels, not design
tokens.

So `packages/bodymap/test/contrast.test.ts` carries its own 15-line
`relativeLuminance` + `contrast` helper. That is the drift this repo exists to
prevent, and the correct fix is the one the house rules name: **extend the shared
package**, exporting a bare `contrastRatio(fg, bg)` from `@broberg/theme` so
every consumer measures WCAG the same way. Filed rather than done inline —
`components` owns `@broberg/theme`, so this is our own gap to close.
