# F044.2 — Six corrections are an ordinary Danish word AND a mishearing

**Status:** Backlog · **Epic:** F044 (`@broberg/speech-dictionary`) · **Written:** 2026-09-07
**Found while:** answering a cms report about a *different* package.

## How it surfaced

cms reported that `@broberg/ai-sdk`'s `applyPronunciations` refuses to match
across a hyphen, so `AI-agenter` (13×) and `AI-native` (12×) were read aloud as
one mangled word. Not our package — but comparing the two rules turned up a
defect in ours, in the opposite direction.

```
@broberg/ai-sdk           (?<![\w-]) … (?![\w-])          hyphen BLOCKS a match
@broberg/speech-dictionary (?<![\p{L}\p{N}_]) … (?![\p{L}\p{N}_])   hyphen IS a boundary
```

## The defect

The entries are **mishearings** — the wrong forms a transcriber emits — not
dictionary words. Six of the 59 are *also* ordinary Danish words, and that is the
whole fault: **inside a compound the rule assumes the mishearing while the text
means the word.**

| `from` | → | in a compound |
|---|---|---|
| `ruter` | `router` | **API-ruter** → API-router *(a network device)* |
| `mærke` | `merge` | varemærke- → vare-merge- |
| `passe` | `parse` | |
| `bogen` | `buggen` | |
| `kloster` | `cluster` | |
| `dansen` | `dashboardet` | |

## Measured on real Danish — by cms, not by me

628,013 characters across four sites and 15 collections. All 59 terms,
occurrences after a hyphen:

```
ruter  2   "**14 biblioteksfiler og 6 API-ruter** brugte alle process.env.CM…"
           "…kalder direkte på interne CMS API-ruter — ikke MCP — for lavere laten…"
every other term   0
```

And the column a counter cannot produce, which they read by hand: **both are the
correct Danish reading.** `API-ruter` is the plural of *rute* — endpoints in an
API. A real false positive, twice, in their corpus, today.

### My first framing was wrong, and their measurement is why

I told them the risk was *"19 terms of 2–5 letters that could sit after a
hyphen"*. **Four of the five I named hit zero.** The dangerous property is not
shortness — it is **being both readings at once**.

## The caveat that keeps this open (theirs)

> *"Jeg måler SKREVET indhold. Jeres pakke retter TALE-TIL-TEKST."*

A mishearing arises in the speech stream, not in an article. Their corpus says
which hyphenated compounds exist in Danish tech writing and **nothing** about
what a transcriber emits. `API-ruter` is strong precisely because it is both —
a phrase we write *and* one someone would say.

And zero in a *software* corpus is not zero in Danish: `vare-mærke`,
`regnskabs-bogen`, `kirke-kloster` would all fire, in a kind of text neither of
us has. They said so themselves rather than letting two hits read as a green
light for the language.

## The general finding

**Same axis, two packages, opposite defaults, and both need per-entry control.**
ai-sdk refuses to cross a hyphen and misses `AI-agenter`; we cross it and break
`API-ruter`. Neither default is right for every word — only the word's owner can
say, which is the argument for a per-entry flag over one global rule.

## Scope

A per-entry opt-out, defaulting to today's behaviour, set on the six.

### Non-goals

- **Not changing the global rule.** Correct for 53 of 59 and for every
  abbreviation; tightening it would trade a measured 2-occurrence fault for an
  unmeasured loss of every legitimate compound match.
- **Not deleting the six entries.** They are real mishearings; removing them
  re-opens the fault they exist to fix. The compound is the exception.

## Reuse

None — this is `@broberg/speech-dictionary`'s own matcher. The sibling question
in `@broberg/ai-sdk` is theirs to decide; I sent them the same measurement.
