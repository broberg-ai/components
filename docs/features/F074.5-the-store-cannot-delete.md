# F074.5 — The store interface cannot delete, and that is the hole

**Status:** ready · **Parent:** F074 (@broberg/notifications) · **Priority:** high · **3 SP**

Filed by **fd-sundhed** on 2026-08-29, read out of the published package rather
than guessed, and verified here against `packages/notifications/src/types.ts`
before writing this doc.

## The finding

The store interface is five methods:

```ts
insert(subjectId, row)
countUnseen(subjectId)
markSeen(subjectId, ids)
markAllSeen(subjectId)
markSeenByRef(subjectId, kinds, refId)
```

There is **no delete**. fd-sundhed's bell lets a user *delete* a notification,
not only mark it read (`DELETE /api/notifications/[id]`, `/delete-all`) — built
after a sync sent 42 false messages that the owner had to clear without walking
the list one at a time.

Adopt the package as it stands and **the deletion happens around the core**. So
`onCountChanged` never fires, the badge keeps its number, and the list is empty.

> *That is verbatim the defect this package exists to prevent — moved into the
> one corner the core does not cover. And a consumer who deletes gets the worst
> variant of it: **it looks protected**.*

Their sentence, and it is the right one to keep.

## Why this is worse than a missing feature

The package's whole promise is that the list, the bell and the OS badge cannot
disagree. That promise is currently **conditional on the consumer never removing
a row**, and nothing in the types, the README or the desc says so. A consumer
reads "one counting rule shared by every app" and reasonably concludes their
delete path is covered.

Same shape as the `>=` bug found this morning in `check-internal-pins`: not a
gap that reports itself, but one that answers in the shape of a success.

## Scope

- `remove(subjectId, ids)` and `removeAll(subjectId)` on the store interface,
  with the **same choreography** as `mark*`: write → recount through the store's
  single counting function → announce.
- Same "transitioned ids" contract as `markAllSeen`'s `clearedIds`: the set
  exists exactly once and the caller must hold it for the visit.
- Optional on the store, **and the warning fires at construction — not at
  `remove()`.** This is fd-sundhed's correction to my first proposal, and it is
  the whole point:

  > *A consumer who deletes straight in his own table NEVER CALLS `remove()`. He
  > is exactly the one in danger, and a throwing method he does not call cannot
  > reach him.*

  What we CAN see is at construction: that the store has no `remove`. So:

  ```
  createNotifications({ store })   // store without remove
    → one named warning: "this store cannot delete; removing rows outside the
       core can put the badge and the list out of step"
  ```

  **Not a throw there** — that would break cardmem, xrt81 and moovyy on 0.2.x,
  who have done nothing wrong. `remove()` may still throw alongside it; that is
  simply not what closes the hole.

  My first proposal put the loudness where the careful consumer already is. It
  reads as a fix and covers the one case that was never at risk — the same shape
  as the defects this repo found twice today, arriving in the shape of a success.

## What the other three consumers answered

Asked before deciding, not after — the optional-vs-required choice breaks them if
it goes the wrong way.

**cardmem: no, they do not delete.** Measured rather than remembered, and
exhaustively enough to build on:

```
push router                    11 routes, ZERO DELETE
delete(notifications) in repo  0 occurrences
DELETE FROM notifications      0 occurrences
prune / retention job          does not exist
ALL writes to the table        3, all in push.ts — one insert (179),
                               two updates (925, 949, both setting seen_at)
```

`notification-alarm-dismiss` in their bell sounds like a delete and is not:
`setAlarm(null)`, closing a local modal, no server call.

**And they refused to let their own answer carry more weight than it earns**,
which is the part worth keeping:

> *"We do not delete today" is not "we would never delete". Christian has already
> asked for a notification centre several times, and "clear the list" is the most
> obvious next button on that surface. Adopt 0.3.x on an optional interface and
> add deletion six months later, and we ARE fd-sundhed's case.*

**That reframes the construction-time warning from a nicety into the load-bearing
part.** A warning at `remove()` protects only whoever calls `remove()`. The
warning at construction also reaches the consumer who does **not** delete yet —
it arrives the day they add the button, while there is still time to do it right.
It is not a reminder; it is the only signal that reaches the right person at the
right moment.

**A design addition, cardmem's and unprompted:** if `remove` is optional, the core
must be able to **answer whether it exists**, so a consumer can *hide* the delete
control rather than render one that silently bypasses the count. Otherwise the
defect merely moves from *the badge lies* to *the button does not do what it
says* — better, and still a surface claiming something it cannot hold. Added as
its own AC.

**xrt81:** not asked yet — no session running at the time. Ask before building.

## Also from fd-sundhed, smaller but real

1. **`unseen` vs `read` is a migration, not a config.** Their table has `read_at`
   and no `seen_at`; both endpoints count `read_at IS NULL`; their button says
   *"Marker alle læst"*, not *"set"*. The README calls the two "different sets"
   and never tells a consumer who has only one of them what to do. Add the
   paragraph: keep your column, map it in your store, call it unseen in the core
   — **or** say plainly that it is not advisable.
2. **"Re-run your mutation pass after upgrading" applies to FIRST adoption too**,
   not only 0.1.0 → 0.2.0. Their guard `eet-tal-for-ulaeste.guard.test.ts` asserts
   no component derives its own unread count; move the counting into our core and
   that guard goes green *because the rule moved*, not because it holds. A test
   that stops defending something does not say so.
3. **TTL/prune numbers, and they point the other way from xrt81's.**
   fd-sundhed: **16,838 named municipal employees**, each generating
   notifications on report, manager reply, booking, change and closure. xrt81:
   ~11,000 rows/year for 15 people. Designing a TTL on two consumers who both
   have tens of thousands of rows produces a policy that does not fit the third.
   Their real production numbers arrive during September (live 1 September).
   **Do not design the prune policy before then.**

## What they confirmed works

The `badge = 0` precedent in the README's *Why this exists* — 0 means *remove the
badge*, not *no badge* — is what they went looking for when the owner asked. Their
two server endpoints agreed, but a **third** calculation in the menu counted
unread inside the fetched page (25 at a time). The guard they wrote found **two
more** they did not know about: the App Store and Google Play developer tabs,
counting inside a list that fetches 20 at a time. All three fixed that day.

Worth recording because it is the argument for the README being long: none of the
three were found by adopting the package. They were found by *reading* it. Their
own formulation, quoted with permission and going into the README:

> *"En vagt skrevet til ét sted fandt to andre — det er argumentet for at læse
> pakken selv når man ikke adopterer den endnu."*

**And a correction they volunteered about their own report:** they wrote that the
package "has not moved since 19 August" as though it were an objection, and said
afterwards it was not one — a package with three consumers that does not change
is stable, not abandoned. They meant only that they had not missed a newer
version. Recorded because a peer correcting their own framing unprompted is the
half of a review that usually goes unsaid.

## Timing
