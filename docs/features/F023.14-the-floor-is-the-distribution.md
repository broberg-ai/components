# F023.14 — `readableAccent` stops at the threshold, so the floor IS the distribution

**Status:** Backlog · **Epic:** F023 (`@broberg/mail-core`) · **Written:** 2026-09-06
**Filed by:** cms, from their own call sites. Swept across the colour space here.

## What I proposed, and why cms's answer was better

After shipping 0.8.0 I told cms their fallback red at 4.60:1 was worth a note —
a colour sitting 0.10 above the floor, where a cosmetic tweak would push it
under AA without anyone noticing. I framed it as an edge case.

They measured all four sites in their registry plus the fallback, through
their *own* call site (`emailAccentColor` from site-config, i.e. what customers
actually receive):

```
site            accent    cta label   as TEXT (card)   (footer #f4f4f5)
webhouse-site   #F7BB2E   10.03       #94701c  4.57    #886719  4.78
sanneandersen   #F7BB2E   10.03       #94701c  4.57    #886719  4.78
trail           #F7BB2E   10.03       #94701c  4.57    #886719  4.78
broberg-ai      #00b2ff    7.30       #007db3  4.58    #0074a6  4.72
(fallback)      #e4203a    4.60       #e4203a  4.60    #d91e37  4.56
```

**8 of 10 text measurements between 4.56 and 4.78.** Their conclusion, and it
disproves my own suggestion: *a warning about proximity to the floor would fire
on everything, and therefore on nothing.*

## Confirmed here, over the whole colour space

The cause is one line in `readableAccent`:

```ts
if (r >= 4.5) return h;   // returns the FIRST step that clears the floor
```

It walks 40 steps of 2.5% and returns the moment it crosses. So the output sits
just above the threshold **by construction** — not because of which brands we
happen to have.

Swept an 8-step grid over the RGB cube against the footer backdrop; 21,603
colours needed adjusting at all:

```
4.50–4.60    8,068   37.3%
4.60–4.75   10,574   48.9%
4.75–5.00    2,961   13.7%
median 4.63 · p90 4.77 · max 4.90
```

Not one result exceeds 4.90. cms's ten points are not a sample of a wider
spread; they *are* the distribution.

## The finding: one package, two roles, two different margins

```
the cta LABEL   picks the better of two FIXED inks    →  7.30 – 10.03
derived TEXT    walks until it just clears the floor  →  4.50 –  4.90
```

The role with headroom is the one that needed none. The role carrying small
type at the bottom of a mail, in whatever a client decides to render it as, has
none at all.

Neither of us saw this before their table, and it is not visible from either
side alone: I had the sweep and no call sites, they had the call sites and no
sweep.

## The genuine edge case is the opposite of the one I proposed

Not *"close to the floor"* — everything is. It is **close to the floor AND
untouchable**.

cms's fallback `#e4203a` measures 4.60 as text and is returned **unchanged**,
because 4.60 ≥ 4.5. It is what they use when a site-config says nothing, so it
is the configuration reaching the most recipients and the one nobody chose. A
future *"let's warm the red up a little"* takes it under AA on every such mail,
and the package would say nothing — `readableAccent` returns its input verbatim
and reports no number at all.

## Scope

- **Decide first.** Aiming above the floor changes what customers see on every
  mail from a light-brand consumer. That is a product call, not a lint fix.
- If yes: a target (aim 5.5, hard floor 4.5), re-swept.
- Address the untouchable band, or state why not.
- A release report naming each consumer's OLD and NEW derived colour.

### Non-goals

- **Not lowering the cta label.** The asymmetry argues for raising the derived
  text, never for levelling down the role that is already safe.
- **Not re-opening byte-identity for dark brands.** `#0f7391` is returned
  untouched today and must stay so. A target applies to colours the function
  already adjusts.
- **Not a proximity warning.** Measured dead on arrival — see above.

## Not urgent

No illegible mail is in production. All five of cms's colours clear AA in both
roles today. This buys margin against what we do not control: small type, a
client's own re-rendering, a footer backdrop someone lightens later.

## Reuse

None — this is `@broberg/mail-core`'s own derivation, in the repo that owns it.
Checked Discovery for an existing contrast/colour primitive before filing:
`@broberg/theme` owns design tokens, not runtime contrast derivation, and
nothing else in the roster computes WCAG ratios.
