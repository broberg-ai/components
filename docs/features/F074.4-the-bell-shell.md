# F074.4 — The bell shell

**Christian, directly:** *"det er dumt at 4 løsninger anvender det samme setup UDEN
et genbrugeligt komponent."*

> **Written before the consumers answered, rewritten after.** Two of the four
> replied within the hour and between them changed the design three times. The
> first draft of this document would have shipped a shell that was wrong for
> cardmem on day one. What follows is the corrected version; the corrections are
> marked, because *what I had assumed* is the useful part for whoever reads this
> next.

## The epic said the menu is not the shared part. It was right about the row.

F074's non-goals list reads:

> **The list UI.** Three consumers, three brands, three row shapes. A shared
> renderer becomes a prop-soup nobody dares change, and it is the half that is
> cheap to write and expensive to share.

That argument is about the **row**, and it still holds. It says nothing about the
**shell** — and every defect below is shell, and none of them is cheap.

## First: a premise I stated to Christian and got wrong

I told him cardmem and xrt81 run `@broberg/notifications` in production. cardmem
corrected it within the hour, and Discovery's enrollments endpoint agrees:

```
@broberg/notifications  →  moovyy  0.2.0  uses     ← the only one
```

cardmem greps to **zero hits** across their repo and counts server-side by hand
(`apps/server/src/api/push.ts:143`). xrt81 shows no enrollment either.

**So the counting core was designed from cardmem's and xrt81's production
measurements, and neither of them adopted it.** xrt81 wrote the sentence that
predicted what that costs:

> *v0.1.0's only production experience will come from the consumer who cannot
> reveal a parity error.*

moovyy has no old counter to disagree with. Eleven days of single-consumer use
that can prove the core **works** and can never prove it **agrees**.

This matters for THIS card, not just as an admission: a shell that lands on top of
a core two of four consumers never took has the same problem one layer up. For
cardmem, adopting the shell means **migrating the counter too** — they said so
plainly. Scope to plan for, not a surprise to discover.

## The defects the shell must be born owning

### 1. Two bells, two counts — xrt81

They render the bell in a mobile topbar **and** a desktop bar, with the count
*inside* the component. Two independent numbers. «Markér alle læst» zeroed one of
them, and the bell sat at **2** with every row read.

*(Relayed by cardmem from xrt81's `b1c83c6`; confirmation requested from xrt81
directly rather than taken second-hand.)*

**The invariant:** one provider owns count + list + panel · the bell is a pure
trigger with **no state of its own** · the panel renders **once**.

No consumer invents the test *"two bells, one panel"* unprompted.

### 2. A portalled panel cannot use `contains()` — cardmem, measured

They portal the panel to `document.body` to escape the topbar's
`backdrop-filter`, and closed on a document-level `pointerdown` guarded by
`rootRef.contains(target)`.

Portalled, **the panel is outside `rootRef`** — so every click *inside* it was
judged "outside" and closed the panel before the row's `onClick` could run. Every
desktop click swallowed. Lens caught it; a manual phone test likely would not.

**The test must portal.** With panel and trigger under one parent this bug is
invisible.

> **Already acted on.** The reuse check for this card found that
> `@broberg/ui-controls-core`'s `isOutsideAll` takes an **array** of elements —
> correct for a portalled panel, and better than what all four apps hand-rolled.
> It had one hole: the capture-phase scroll listener never asked *where* the
> scroll came from, so a panel with a scrolling list closed itself. Fixed and
> shipped as `0.1.1` (F016.7) before this shell needed it.
>
> cardmem is currently immune to that hole **because they do not use the
> primitive** — they close on a full-viewport backdrop's `onClick`, and a scroll
> produces no click. Worth saying out loud: adopting the shell moves them from
> *immune by construction* to *depending on the hole being closed*. It is closed.

### 3. No keyboard, anywhere — cardmem, measured

A grep across all 419 lines for
`Escape|onKeyDown|keydown|.focus()|tabIndex|autofocus`: **zero hits.** No focus
trap, no Escape, in the panel *or* in the alarm modal — so they have a
`role="alertdialog"` demanding acknowledgement with **no keyboard path to it**.

Also missing: `aria-haspopup` and `aria-controls` on the trigger (nothing points
at the panel), `aria-modal` on either layer (the background is never announced
inert), and `aria-labelledby` against a real heading.

They are deliberately **not** fixing it locally, so there will not be two
implementations of the thing being centralised.

### 4. TWO STACKED DIALOGS — cardmem *(this is the one I had not designed for)*

```
const onRow = (n) => {
  if (isAlarm(n.kind)) { setAlarm(n); return; }   // ← no setOpen(false)
  ...
}
```

So an alarm row opens the modal **on top of the still-open panel**:

```
backdrop z-60 · panel z-61   role="dialog"
backdrop z-70 · alarm z-71   role="alertdialog"
```

**The shell cannot assume one dialog at a time.** A naive focus trap on the panel
fights the alarm's. The requirement is the **stack**: which layer owns focus, and
where focus goes when the top closes — **back to the row in the panel, not to the
bell**.

### 5. ESCAPE IS AN OUTCOME, NOT A CLOSE — cardmem

| layer | Escape |
|---|---|
| panel | close. Uncontroversial. |
| alarm | **???** |

The alarm modal exists to **require** acknowledgement. Their two buttons are
`ack` and `dismiss` — two outcomes, not one close.

> **A shell that hardcodes "Escape closes" lets a user dismiss an incident alarm
> with one keypress and nothing recorded. That is not an a11y detail; it is
> whether the alarm means anything.**

So `onEscape` returns an outcome the consumer decides, with *close* as the default
**only for non-modal layers**.

### 6. Anchoring happens at the instant of opening — cardmem

The same panel is a **bottom sheet** on mobile (`fixed`, left/right 0, bottom 0)
and an **anchored dropdown** on desktop, positioned from
`bellRef.getBoundingClientRect()` **at open time**.

If the shell owns open/close it must either own the anchoring or hand it off
explicitly. **It cannot be indifferent**, because the anchoring happens in the
same moment as the opening.

### 7. Every string must be injectable — fd-sundhed

> *"Vi kan ikke tage en pakke der siger «Mark all read» på en dansk skærm."*

Danish and English. «Markér alle læst», the empty state, "see all" — **and every
`aria-label`**. The labels get translated because they are visible; the
screen-reader text is the one nobody notices is still English.

### 8. The empty panel is the COMMON case — both

fd-sundhed measured it: an employee with no cases has no notifications, and that
is **the most frequent state in their system**, not an edge.

cardmem's `notifications-empty` has **zero focusable elements** — so a focus trap
that assumes at least one crashes on the most ordinary screen there is.

Two consumers, two different reasons, one screen. It has its own criterion.

## Deliberately not shared

- **The row** — `renderRow`. cardmem's project chip is *data*, not decoration
  (their notifications cross projects).
- **The "see all" route** — a prop. cardmem's dropdown is `limit=10` with a full
  `/notifications` page behind it.
- **Grouping and pagination** — nobody does either.
- **A TTL** — the epic already refused one built on an assumption.
- **`overscroll-behavior: none`** — fd-sundhed's own CSS, for a fixed bottom bar
  the panel opens over. Not our code, but it goes in **our README**: the
  rubber-band float is only reproducible on an iPhone in standalone, and the
  fourth consumer hits it blind if we stay quiet.

## Open, with a decision rule rather than a guess

**Does the row outcome contract belong in the core or in a prop?**

| consumer | acknowledge-without-navigating? |
|---|---|
| cardmem | **yes** — `ALARM_KINDS = {incident, alarm, critical}` |
| fd-sundhed | **no**, measured — 42 rows across 3 kinds, **every one has a link** |
| xrt81 | asked |
| moovyy | asked |

**If 2 of 4 have it, the contract belongs in the core; if only cardmem does, it is
a prop.** Not guessing before the other two answer.

Note that fd-sundhed argues the outcome belongs at the row *regardless*: their
rows lead to **different places** (the employee's case vs the manager's approval
screen), and "navigate and close" can be wrong on a phone when the user came to
see the list. That argument holds with nobody having alarms.

## Architecture — headless core, thin wrappers

cardmem's recommendation, and their reasoning is better than "fewer entry points":

> everything valuable in the shell (portal, outside-click, focus trap, Escape) is
> exactly the framework-entangled part — and exactly the part that carried our bug.

Two parallel implementations of *that* is the same two-houses-one-truth pattern
this package exists to end. So: a framework-agnostic state machine, with `/react`
and `/preact` as ~50-line wrappers. fd-sundhed's Next.js then costs a wrapper, not
a fork.

The wrappers are asserted against the **same** behaviour suite, not two
hand-written ones — F052.22's lesson, where two renderers over one core disagreed
because only one of them called it, and what caught it was a criterion worded
*"asserted in each, separately"*.

## How this must be verified

**cardmem's warning is the sharpest thing said about testing this:**

> focus, Tab order and Escape are **user-agent behaviour**. happy-dom will report
> **green** on a trap that catches nothing.

So the trap is driven through **Lens, in a real engine**: Tab through, and assert
`document.activeElement` never leaves the layer.

**And the negative control is mandatory, not recommended:** a focusable element
rendered **behind** the modal must be unreachable. Without it the trap passes by
accident on exactly the kind of minimal fixture you write to test it — which
cardmem names as the mistake they have made most often this year.

Their testids are stable and are what Lens drives:
`topbar-notifications` · `topbar-notifications-count` · `notifications-backdrop` ·
`notification-row` · `notifications-mark-all` · `notifications-see-all` ·
`notifications-empty` · `notification-alarm-modal` · `notification-alarm-ack` ·
`notification-alarm-dismiss`

## The strongest evidence the shell is shareable

cardmem offered it unprompted: they **already** share their row between the bell
dropdown and the full page (F175.4/F175.5), specifically so *"a notification looks
and behaves the same in both places"*. They ran the experiment internally, on
their own brand, and it held.

## A finding that outlives this card

cardmem's keyboard gap passed **all four** of their review pillars green. Their
own breakdown of why is worth keeping:

```
ac        checks what the CARD claimed. Nobody writes "and it works with a keyboard".
code      reads the diff. ABSENT CODE IS NOT IN A DIFF.
security  looks at data paths. A focus trap is not a data path.
lens      the DOM critic measures GEOMETRY and CONTRAST — occlusion, overlap,
          clipping, off-viewport. It never asks whether an element can be REACHED.
```

Four pillars answering *is it correct · is it safe · does it look right · did you
do what you said*. **None answers whether a human can operate it.** Every pillar
checks something that **is there**; a missing affordance is invisible to all four
**by construction**.

Filed by them as cardmem-F095.24, advisory before blocking — *"a gate that MUST be
worked around teaches everyone to work around it."*

## Reuse

- **`@broberg/notifications`** — the layer directly beneath. The shell calls
  `markAllSeen()` / `unseenCount()`; it counts nothing itself. fd-sundhed's
  defence of that design is better than the plan's own: they do not need to
  subtract anything today, but they have an undecided question (does a fully
  muted type still show in the bell?), and delegation means they can change their
  mind **without touching the shell**.
- **`@broberg/ui-controls-core`** — adopted, and extended rather than worked
  around (F016.7, above).
- **`@broberg/webpush`** — not imported. `onCountChanged` is the seam.

## Non-goals

- Re-implementing the counting core. F074.1's choreography stands.
- Owning the alarm modal itself. The shell hands off; what the consumer renders is
  theirs.
- A design system. This ships behaviour and structure, not a look.

## What moovyy answered — and the premise of theirs I had wrong

Asked 2026-08-29, alongside the delete question. They measured rather than
remembered, and corrected us on our own claim about them.

### The poster that never existed

This epic said each consumer named what they will not give up, and listed
**"moovyy the film poster"**. Measured by them in `app.js:3107-3113`: their row is
a **dot (`akt-prik`) plus text** — title, optional subtitle, relative time. **No
image, no icon tile.** So a reason not to share the shell in August was invented
here, published to the whole fleet in the Discovery roster, and read as fact.

Their conclusion, which is the useful one: *they would take the shell as it
stands, without `renderRow`.* Keep the prop for the others, not for them.

Corrected in `scripts/inventory-data.mjs` the same day.

### The badge is driven in the same call as the number

```js
function visTal(n) {
  klokketal.hidden = !n;
  klokketal.replaceChildren(tekst(n > 99 ? '99+' : String(n)));
  if ('setAppBadge' in navigator) (n ? navigator.setAppBadge(n) : navigator.clearAppBadge()).catch(() => {});
}
```

And the number always comes from the server — `/api/aktivitet` returns the list
**and** `ulæste`; the surface never counts. *"With two counters they disagree the
day one of them is fixed."*

**Consequence for the shell:** whatever owns the count must own the badge or hand
out a hook. Otherwise badge and bell drift — which is xrt81's F074.27 exactly.

### Two things they rebuilt, and both are shell behaviour

**(a) "Mark all read" used to close and reopen the panel.** Two defects at once:
it flickered in the face of the person who had just tapped, and the order of DOM
changes became dependent on *when a network call answered*. Now the contents are
redrawn while the panel stays put. **A shared shell must redraw, never re-mount.**

**(b) `replaceChildren` turns a `null` child into the literal text "null"** and
writes it into the header. It was in production on 19 August: the panel showed
the word *"null"* where the button belongs. **Every Lens anchor was green** —
only the screenshot showed it. This lands on a shell directly, because *"Mark all
read"* exists only when there ARE unread, which makes it precisely the optional
child.

### Focus: we win here, so do not design as if they had it

Measured: the only `keydown` listener handles Escape; no initial focus, no trap,
no focus restored on close. **Do not treat their behaviour as a constraint** — the
shell is a straight gain for them.

### clearOn — a second consumer independently refuses clear-on-open

They clear on **interaction**, never on open. Opening the panel changes nothing; a
row click marks that row seen (and only if it was not already — otherwise the
response is empty and the call did nothing); "Mark all read" is explicit.

> *We actively do NOT want a shell that clears on open. "A new film has landed" is
> the entire reason for the visit; deleting it because the panel was opened
> removes the only signal about what happened while he was away.*

That is the same refusal already recorded against `clearOn` as core config,
arriving now from the consumer who originally proposed it.

### clearedIds: they do not have the concept, and their advice is better for it

After "Mark all read" they redraw from the **server's** response, so the grey
comes from `setAt` and not from local state — it survives any render because it is
not local. A row click navigates away and closes the panel, so a highlight never
has to survive anything.

> *If the shell holds `clearedIds` for the visit, lay it ON TOP of the server's
> state, not instead of it. Otherwise a redraw from fresh server data loses it —
> or worse, overwrites it.*

### The alarm-outcome tally

**moovyy: NO** (measured). Their five kinds — `ny-film`, `ønske-foreslået`,
`ønske-godkendt`, `hentning-fejlede`, `sig-til-svaret` — all navigate or do
nothing. No alertdialog, no acknowledgement.

So on evidence: **cardmem 1, moovyy 0**, fd-sundhed and xrt81 outstanding. The
decision rule was 2-of-4 → core.

**But they separated their opinion from their measurement, and said to take the
outcome contract into the core anyway:** *"click → navigate and close" is an
assumption that is cheap to avoid now and expensive to roll back later, once three
consumers have built on top of it.* Marked as opinion by them, recorded as
opinion here. It does not move the tally; it is an argument about asymmetric cost,
and it is a good one.

### Where the NEXT shared thing is

Unprompted: the core writes to **one** subject, so fanning out to every user is
each consumer's own code. moovyy built `tilAlle` and `tilEjeren` on top of their
store, plus `tælUlæste` / `liste` / `skriv` and their own `sendBadge` hook.

> *Not a complaint; an observation about where the next shared thing is.* Four
> consumers will each write that fan-out again.

Not carded yet — it is an observation from one consumer, and the same discipline
that produced this card says ask the others first.

### And they did not take back the uncomfortable part

They had no old counter to compare against, so their adoption can prove the core
*works* and never that it *agrees*. Their words: *"we do not take that back by
saying it has just been running" — as it has.*

### Two sharpenings they sent back, both against my own wording

**"Redraw, never re-mount" — assert the ORDER, not only the identity.**

> *Our bug was not that the node was replaced; it was that the REPLACEMENT
> happened in response to a network call, so the DOM changed in whatever order
> the network decided. The same node identity before and after can hold while the
> contents are still drawn TWICE because two calls answered. Assert that there is
> ONE redraw per action.*

My AC was weaker than the defect it was written from. Corrected: the fixture now
has two in-flight responses, and the assertion counts redraws.

**"An absent child renders nothing" — drive the API the shell actually uses.**

> *Our trap was specifically `replaceChildren(...)` with a `null` in the list.
> That turns `null` into the TEXT «null». `appendChild(null)` THROWS — and a test
> written against it would be green on the dangerous path.*

That is the same failure as *a test green for the wrong reason*: the convenient
fixture is accidentally safe. Corrected: through the shell's real render path.

## What xrt81 answered — all four consumers are now measured, not assumed

### The citation was wrong, and they corrected it

This card cited **b1c83c6** as the two-bells defect. It is the **FIX**:
*"fix(F071.5): ét tal for alle klokker — to instanser gav to sandheder"*, landed
11 August. The bug was real and exactly as described — Lens caught it, the bell
read 2 with all three rows read — but it is **"xrt81 hit it and fixed it"**, not a
standing defect in their product.

Worth being precise about, because I had written a defect in *someone else's*
code into this card as a measurement when it was a **relay**, and the card itself
said confirmation had been requested and never arrived.

**Their post-fix shape is verbatim the invariant this card asserts**, which is the
strongest confirmation available: `NotificationsProvider` owns unseen + items +
open; `NotificationBell` is 26 lines with **zero** `useState`; `NotificationSheet`
renders **once** in the Shell. Their own test locks it: the button may not have
state, the shell must show >1 bell and exactly 1 panel.

### THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST TODAY — a live bug, and it is about our promise

> `countUnseen` **filters** muted categories (via `pushPrefs`). `listNotifications`
> **does not**.

**They corrected their own report within the hour, and the correction matters** —
I had already written the strong version into an AC as a present-tense fact.

Measured in their production (bun:sqlite directly on the Fly volume):

```
30 members, 1 has any push_prefs row   Robert Andersen, {"chatAll":false}
chat_all is the LARGEST unseen type    164 of 221 unseen rows in the whole base
his own unseen with navigate           3 x chat_all (muted) + 1 x message (not)
```

**What he actually sees today: the bell says 1, the panel highlights 4.** The
disagreement is real and live.

**But the button is NOT dead for him.** It is `disabled={unseen === 0}`, and his
one un-muted `message` holds the count at 1. The lock-out needs *all* his unseen
to be muted — so it is **one click away, not happening now**: read that one
message and he has 3 highlighted rows, a bell of 0, and a dead button.

> *I gave you the strong version of the symptom; production shows the mild one.
> Build the AC on what was measured, not on my phrasing.*

**And the distribution is the design input.** 1 of 30 here; cardmem had 2 users
with a prefs row. Across three repos, **fewer than 5 users in total**. Muted is an
EDGE, not the norm — which is exactly the state that is expensive to test your way
to and cheap to get free from the shell, and it breaks the screen completely for
whoever is in it.

They replaced their own AC with our wording on their card (F071.7), because it is
the more general one: it holds for disagreements neither of us has found yet.

**This is a fourth way the list and the bell can disagree, and our core does
nothing about it** — because the core deliberately does not own the count query
(the muted rule is the entire reason) and does not own the list query either. The
package's promise is *"the list, the bell and the badge cannot disagree"*, and
that promise has a hole exactly where the two queries are both the consumer's.

The count and the list **answer different questions by design**. So the fix is not
to make them equal — that would break the muted rule. It is that **a control which
clears the list must be enabled by what is VISIBLE, never by the number** — which
covers both the state they are in today and the one they are a click from. Added
as AC.
