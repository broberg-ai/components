# F079.10 — The estimate cannot see the tool schemas, and the config asks a question no human can answer

> Story under **F079 — `@broberg/chat`**. Follows F079.9 (history management), reported by **cms** on the day they swapped onto the package in production.

## Where this came from

cms, relaying Christian: *"valgene her er ikke CMS-specifikke, de er generiske for modulet, og de bør stå hos jer så næste forbruger ikke skal genopfinde dem."*

Two faults in the same seam. **The first must land before the second, or the second hides it.**

## ⚠️ 1. The guard under-counts, in the green direction

Measured in our own source rather than taken on their word:

```ts
estimate?: (messages: ChatMessage[], system?: string) => number;
prepareHistory(messages, config, system)
```

**No tool specs anywhere.** So the number the ceiling is compared against omits the largest fixed cost a real consumer has.

cms measured theirs over 64 tools:

| | |
|---|---|
| names | 967 chars |
| descriptions | 8,543 |
| input schemas | 18,756 |
| **total** | **28,266 chars ≈ 8,300 tokens, on every call** |

Two thirds is JSON schema, not prose, and **it grows every time somebody adds a tool.**

> The guard therefore says there is room while the provider is already over — and the failure lands as the dead conversation F079.9 exists to prevent, on the consumer who did everything we asked.

**The fix is the one that cannot be forgotten, not the one that is cheaper.** cms offered us both: thread the tool specs into the calculation, or document that the consumer subtracts their own overhead. Documentation is cheaper and it is the option that fails silently for the next reader. Prefer *unrepresentable* over *documented* — the same call as `defineTool()` throwing rather than explaining that `permission` is required.

## 2. The config asks in a unit nobody decides in

`{ strategy, maxInputTokens }` is two numbers. The question a person actually answers is:

> *What happens when the conversation gets too long — and how long may it get, when cms authors multilingual content for hours while a visitor at sanne's asks three questions?*

One product question, two numeric fields, and we leave the translation to every consumer. **Christian answered it in two seconds once cms put it in his language.** It could not have been asked in tokens.

### The three options, in cms's framing

| | |
|---|---|
| **Summarise the oldest** (`compact`) | the thread is preserved; costs one extra model call when it fires. Right when someone builds over a long session and refers back to something agreed early. |
| **Forget the oldest** (`window`) | free and instant — **but what goes is usually the user's OPENING INSTRUCTION** (tone, language, role), and the conversation carries on without it while everything looks normal. That hidden cost is not in our docs today. |
| **No limit** | named as a **choice**, not as the absence of one. It is what every consumer has if they skip the field, and the consequence is our own F079.9 rationale: the conversation does not get expensive, it **dies**, and a retry resends the same oversized payload for ever. |

So: `history: "long-authoring" | "standard" | "visitor-qa"`, resolving to numbers **we** are responsible for, with the raw object still there for anyone who wants to steer. *"About 150 exchanges"* is a decision a person can take. *"96,000 tokens"* is not.

An unknown profile name **throws**, naming the valid ones. A fallback to a default would be a silent choice about somebody's bill.

## The Danish rate: evidence, not a default

cms measured **3.41 chars/token on their own Danish prose**, against our documented ~4. That is exactly the direction we warned them about, now with a number.

It goes in the README **attributed and dated**, beside the default — not as our constant. One consumer's corpus is not the fleet's, and a measured example that shows *why* to inject your own tokenizer is worth more than a second number pretending to be universal.

## What cms could NOT measure, recorded as their caveat

They could not run the empirical ceiling test: **there is no Mistral key outside production.** Their limit is *derived* — 128k documented context, minus max output, minus 8,300 schema, minus 20 % margin — and they recorded it as an assumption with its date, with the measurement outstanding until it can run where the key lives.

They flagged this themselves so we would not cite it as a measurement. We do not.

## Non-goals

- Not changing the default `estimateTokens` rate on one consumer's corpus.
- Not a tokenizer. `estimate` stays injectable; that is the escape hatch and it is the right one.
- Not measuring provider ceilings for consumers. We cannot hold a key per model per repo, and a table without dates rots — the method belongs in the README, the number belongs to whoever runs it.
