# F052.31 — The hint cannot be turned off, and in fullscreen it falls off the phone

**Christian asked for the prop. fd-sundhed measured the defect** — and is running
a second CSS wedge because of it.

## Measured

Production 0.8.0, iPhone 15, WebKit. Exactly one element is clipped:

```
bodymap3d-empty    top 858, bottom 912      window height 852
```

Entirely below the fold. It sits in normal flow inside the panel column; in
fullscreen the canvas takes the full height, the column wraps beneath it, and the
hint is pushed past the edge.

On Christian's own phone it is *partly* visible because his safe area differs —
his screenshot shows the sentence cut in half at the bottom of the black surface.
**It reads as a bug in the app.**

## There is no way to turn it off

```ts
const showEmptyHint = anySelectable || ui?.hoverHint !== undefined;
```

It renders whenever anything is selectable. `ui.hoverHint` changes only the
**text** — and setting it to an empty string produces an **empty bordered box**,
which is worse than the sentence. There is no path to *"do not show this"*.

## This is their SECOND wedge

The first was `chrome.panelBg` painting two surfaces (F052.29, fixed in 0.8.0).
They have now pinned this element with CSS against our own `data-fullscreen`
attribute.

They told us rather than just shipping it — the behaviour we asked for. **A
consumer maintaining wedges against our inline styles is the drift the reuse rule
exists to stop**, and two in two days is a pattern, not an incident.

## Two arguments for handing the hint over entirely

**1. The text is already theirs.** They pass *«Træk for at dreje kroppen · tryk
hvor det gør ondt»* because our default — *«Hover to highlight · click a body
part»* — is **desktop language**. There is no hover on a phone, on a component
whose own documentation calls the phone its primary surface. They have already
taken over the content; they are only missing control of the rest.

**2. Their screen already has its own help line**, at the bottom of the reporting
flow. In fullscreen ours and theirs sit saying nearly the same thing in two
places. With `hint={false}` they say it once, in their own words, where they chose.

## Why `placement` and not just a toggle

**It is what fixes the defect rather than moving it.**

| | right place |
|---|---|
| in the small box | after the canvas |
| in fullscreen | at the bottom of the stage |

A consumer cannot make that call in CSS without knowing our internal structure —
which the current wedge proves, since it targets an attribute we happen to expose.

```tsx
<BodyMap3D hint={false} />
<BodyMap3D hint={{ text: "…", placement: "stage-bottom" }} />
```

## Our own default text is wrong on the primary surface

Separate from the layout, and fixed in the same pass: *"Hover to highlight"*
describes an interaction that **does not exist on a touchscreen**. A default
nobody can turn off *and* that is wrong on phones is two defects sharing one
element.

## Reuse

Discovery searched `hint`, `tooltip`, `empty state`, `coach mark`. Nothing in
`@broberg/*` owns in-component help copy, and nothing should — it belongs to the
component that renders it, or, as here, to the app that knows what else is
already on the screen.

The reuse that applies is internal and is the whole point: the hint goes through
the existing `ui` label object and the da/en locale, so a consumer who has already
translated this component gets this string too — the same rule F052.28 records.

## Non-goals

- A tooltip system, a coach-mark, or first-run onboarding. This is one line of
  copy in a state that already exists.
- Changing where the picker panel sits. Only the hint moves.
- Removing `ui.hoverHint`. It keeps working; it is documented as superseded.
