# F052.20 — Tap a marked point again and it is gone

> Story under **F052 — `@broberg/bodymap`**. Filed by **fd-sundhed** on behalf of their user, decided by Christian, 2026-08-28.

## The ask, in the user's own words

> *«Hvorfor kan jeg ikke bare trykke på punktet igen og det forsvinder?»*

And Christian, restating it himself: *"Man trykker på et bodymap punkt og jeg vil gerne kunne fjerne punktet igen ved at trykke på det samme punkt igen."*

**fd-sundhed did the right thing and it is worth recording:** they did *not* hand-roll it locally. Several apps use this component, so they routed it here as a package extension. That is the reuse rule working rather than being reminded.

## Measured in our source before building

Two separate click paths, and neither knows about the other:

```
react.tsx:528   pickRegion(key)  →  setSelected(key)      // opens the panel
three.tsx:335   if (k) setSelectedRef.current(k)          // opens the panel
```

Both already have a `removePain()` behind a «Fjern» button *inside* the panel. So today: tap a marked point → the panel re-opens → press a second button.

## The trade-off, put to Christian BEFORE building

This removes a capability, and it is a health product, so it was not mine to assume: **today that same tap is the only way to re-open a marked region and change its intensity.**

Three options were offered — remove immediately · first tap opens and second removes · a per-app setting.

**He chose: remove immediately.** A user who wants to change an intensity now clears the point and marks it again. His decision, taken with the cost stated, and it is the simplest and most predictable of the three.

## What is NOT being deleted

**The «Fjern» button stays.** His chosen option called it *"redundant"*, which is not the same as *"delete it"*:

- it is still reachable in the panel immediately after marking a fresh point (the panel stays open on that region);
- it is the **keyboard and screen-reader path**;
- **F052.11 made this component's accessibility a legal requirement** — fd-sundhed's customer is Aalborg Kommune, a public authority under the EU web accessibility directive.

Removing a labelled button to save a line would be a regression in exactly the dimension that is regulated.

## The two things most likely to go wrong

1. **Fixing only one renderer.** `react.tsx` and `three.tsx` share no click code. This repo has recorded twice *today* that a defect in one half of a pair is in both — and once that the sibling branch is the one with no test at all.
2. **A pan mistaken for a tap.** The 2D renderer already suppresses the synthetic click after a pan/pinch (`suppressClick`). That guard used to protect against *opening a panel*. It now protects against **deleting the user's mark**, so it needs its own assertion against a marked region.

## Non-goals

- Not a confirmation dialog. The whole point is one tap; a confirm would restore the two-step it replaces (and native dialogs are banned in this house anyway).
- Not undo. Re-marking is one tap; an undo stack is a bigger feature nobody asked for.
- Not a setting. Christian chose one behaviour for every consumer, deliberately.

## Reuse

Discovery searched for `selection`, `toggle`, `pain map`: nothing in `@broberg/*`
owns selection semantics, and nothing should — "one point per region, latest
wins, tap again to clear" is this component's clinical rule, not a generic
control behaviour.

The reuse that matters here is INTERNAL and is the whole point of the card: the
rule lives once in `decidePick` and BOTH renderers call it. The 2D and 3D click
paths share no other code, and a rule written twice is a rule that drifts —
which is exactly what F052.22 later caught when one half stopped calling it.
